Science-Backed Method

Progression
Rules

How to get stronger, when to push, when to rest, and how to know you're growing.

01
The Double Progression Method
Work within a rep range (e.g. 8–10). Every session, try to add 1 rep to each set without form breakdown. Once you hit the top of your range on all sets, add weight next session (2.5kg for upper, 5kg for lower). Then start again at the bottom of the range with the new weight.
Example: Lat Pulldown 8-10 reps @ 30kg → Hit 10,10 for two sessions → Next session: 32.5kg × 8 reps. Repeat.
02
RPE — Rate of Perceived Exertion
Every working set should end at RPE 8–9 (1–2 reps left in the tank), not RPE 10 (true failure) every time. Reserve true failure for the final set only, or when using drop sets / myo-reps. Going to failure every set increases injury risk and impairs recovery without extra growth benefit.
Phases 1–2 (Weeks 1–16): Stay RPE 7–8. Build form. Phases 3–4 (Weeks 17–52): Push to RPE 9 on final sets.
03
Deload Every 4–6 Weeks
Every 4–6 weeks, take one deload week: drop all weights by 40–50%, keep the same exercises, same reps. This allows connective tissue, joints, and the CNS to recover. Skipping deloads leads to accumulated fatigue masking fitness. You will feel stronger after a deload — this is the process working.
During deload: 3 sets max per exercise, ~50% of normal load. Still go to the gym. Movement heals.
04
Progressive Overload Is Non-Negotiable
Muscle grows only when challenged beyond what it's done before. Track your lifts. Every session should beat the last — even by 1 rep. Use a notes app or paper log. If you've been doing the same weight for 3+ weeks, something is wrong: sleep, food, or effort.
Target: +0.5–1kg per week on major lifts for first 6 months (beginner gains are fast — use this window).
05
Volume Landmarks (MEV → MAV)
Each muscle has a Minimum Effective Volume (MEV) and Maximum Adaptive Volume (MAV). Start at MEV, increase 1–2 sets per muscle per mesocycle (4–6 week block). Signs you've exceeded MAV: persistent soreness 4+ days, decreasing strength, poor sleep. Signs you're below MEV: no pump, no soreness, gains stalled.
Phase 1: 10–12 sets/muscle/week. Phase 2: 12–14. Phase 3–4: 14–16. (Based on Israetel et al., RP Strength)
06
Form First — Always
Ego lifting is the fastest path to zero gains. If your form breaks down, the target muscle is no longer doing the work — other muscles compensate. For the first 12 weeks, record yourself on 1 set per exercise. Compare to technique cues. Form is the skill — treat it as seriously as the weight.
Red flags: excessive lower back arch on press, knees caving on squat, momentum on curls. Film and self-audit monthly.
07
Sleep Is the Anabolic Window
More muscle is built during sleep than during training. Growth hormone peaks during deep sleep. 7–9 hours is the target. Chronically sleeping 6 hours cuts testosterone by ~15% and suppresses muscle protein synthesis. No supplement replaces sleep for muscle growth.
If stressed or poor sleep: reduce training intensity (not volume). Better to maintain than push into fatigue debt.
08
Minimum Rest Between Sets
Research (Schoenfeld 2016) shows 2–3 minute rest for compound lifts, 1–2 min for isolation maximizes strength and hypertrophy. Rushing rest reduces performance on next set, reducing total volume. Use a timer — don't guess. Supersets (antagonists) allow you to rest one muscle while working another.
Hack Squat / Leg Press: 3 min. DB Press, RDL: 2–3 min. Curls, Laterals, Cables: 60–90 sec.
Your Year Ahead

52-Week
Plan

Four phases. One year. From foundation to physique. Aligned with your goal.

Phase 01 of 04
Foundation
Weeks 1 – 12

Build movement quality, neurological efficiency, and the habit of training. Form mastery over load. Beginner gains are fastest here — trust the process. Target: +3–4kg bodyweight.

W 1–2
Orientation & Technique
Learn all 4 workouts. Record form on key lifts. Go to RPE 6–7 only. Priority is movement pattern, not weight.
Orientation
W 3–4
Load Calibration
Find your working weights across all exercises. Use double progression. Begin tracking every session in a notes app.
Establish Baseline
W 5–6
First Progression Block
Add weight wherever you've hit the rep ceiling. Drop sets introduced on isolation lifts (laterals, curls). RPE 8 on final sets.
Progressive Load
W 7
Deload
50% of normal weights. 3 sets max per exercise. Active recovery. Sleep priority week.
Deload
W 8–10
Volume Introduction
Add 1 working set to main compound lifts. Begin myo-reps on bicep curl. Introduce superset pairing on Upper B (incline + T-bar row).
Volume Increase
W 11–12
Peak Phase 1
Push for PRs on main lifts. RPE 9 final sets. Photograph physique at Week 12 for comparison. Note strength gains vs. Week 1.
Peak & Assess
Phase 02 of 04
Accumulation
Weeks 13 – 24

Build the machine. Systematic volume increase, stronger mind-muscle connection. Visible shape changes begin. Target: +3–4kg more bodyweight, visible shoulder width, arm separation.

W 13–14
Phase Reset & Volume Bump
Add 1–2 sets across key muscles. Reassess working weights — you're stronger now. Refilm form check.
New Block Start
W 15–17
Strength + Hypertrophy Emphasis
Keep heavy sets (4-6 reps) on compound lifts. Add isolation intensity techniques: myo-reps on laterals, double drop sets on leg curls.
Intensity Increase
W 18
Deload
Full deload. Optional: replace gym sessions with 30-min walks or light mobility work.
Deload
W 19–22
Maximum Adaptive Volume Block
12–14 sets/muscle/week. All intensity techniques active. Log every session. Chase PRs on hack squat and dumbbell press.
MAV Block
W 23–24
Phase 2 Peak
PR attempts on 5 key lifts. Photograph Week 24. Assess which muscle groups lag — adjust exercise selection for Phase 3.
Peak & Assess
Phase 03 of 04
Intensification
Weeks 25 – 38

Refine the physique. Lower relative volume, higher intensity. Strength numbers surge. Body composition shifts noticeably. Target: clear V-taper development, arm size +1–2 inches.

W 25–26
Exercise Variation Swap
Swap 1–2 exercises per day to address lagging areas. E.g. if upper chest is weak: replace incline Smith with cable incline press. Keep core 4 movements unchanged.
Variation
W 27–30
Strength-First Mesocycle
Heavy sets: 3–5 reps, RPE 9. Drop back-off sets to 6–8 reps. Volume slightly lower. Neuromuscular strength drives long-term size. Prioritize big compound lifts.
Strength Focus
W 31
Deload
After 6 weeks of high intensity — mandatory. Full deload. Prioritize sleep, massage, mobility.
Deload
W 32–36
Hypertrophy Return
Return to 8–12 rep ranges with heavier loads than Phase 2. You're now stronger — same rep range hits the muscle harder. Volume at 14–16 sets/muscle/week.
Loaded Hypertrophy
W 37–38
Phase 3 Peak
All-time PRs attempted. Photograph Week 38. Compare to Week 1. The transformation should be clearly visible at this point.
Peak & Assess
Phase 04 of 04
Refinement
Weeks 39 – 52

Polish the physique. Advanced techniques, weak point specialization, body composition optimization. By Week 52, target physique is largely achieved. Target: 65–68kg lean bodyweight.

W 39–42
Specialization Block
Add 1–2 extra sets for your 2 weakest muscle groups. Everything else maintains. E.g. if arms need work: +1 set bicep, +1 set tricep per upper day.
Specialization
W 43–45
Full Intensity Block
Every set taken to within 1 rep of failure. All intensity techniques (drop sets, myo-reps, supersets) active. This is the highest demand block of the year.
Max Intensity
W 46
Full Deload
Complete rest from loaded training. Light walking, stretching, or yoga only. Let the body consolidate 10+ months of stimulus.
Deload
W 47–50
Peak Shape Block
Return refreshed with significantly higher baseline strength. Chase lifetime PRs. Body composition at its best. Maintain caloric surplus +150–200 kcal over maintenance.
Peak Performance
W 51–52
Year-End Reflection
Final progress photos. Retest 1RM on 3 main lifts. Write your new baseline for Year 2. You've completed the foundation — the real work continues.
